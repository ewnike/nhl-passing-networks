# Decision log and open questions

## Decisions

**D1. Pull from raw GitHub, pinned to a commit.** The downloader does not use statsbombpy. Plain JSON keeps the raw layer identical to the published files, and a pinned SHA gives every teammate the same data. Cost: a manual bump when we want StatsBomb's corrections.

**D2. Canonical coordinates are meters in the acting team's attacking frame.** This removes the need for period and side flips downstream. A hockey adapter must perform that flip itself.

**D3. Minutes played come from the event stream, not the lineup file.** Validation found two real problems in the lineup `positions` data. In match 3754217 (Chelsea v Arsenal) period labels on tactical shift stints are inconsistent, which double counted about 70 seconds, and a player sent off in the first half is listed until the final whistle. In match 3754047 Swansea's starters are labeled "Tactical Shift", so a start flag from the lineup file finds one starter instead of eleven. The adapter now uses Starting XI, Substitution, and red card events (Red Card and Second Yellow). Minutes are real elapsed time including stoppage time.

**D4. Possession ids can step backward by one event.** In one event of 1.3 million (match 3754136) a reception from the earlier possession is logged after the next possession starts. This is a source quirk. Possession construction in step 6 must group by `possession_id`, not rely on contiguous runs. The validator reports it as informational.

**D5. Set pieces dominate raw pass locations.** A first density plot of all passes was dominated by goal kicks, corners, and kickoffs. The exploratory figure now excludes set piece passes. Step 5 should make the open play versus all passes choice explicit and test sensitivity to it.

**D6. Team minute shortfalls are expected after dismissals.** Fifty team matches have fewer than 11 times match duration minutes, against 56 team matches with a dismissal found independently in the raw events. Total minutes above 11 times duration is an error check and passes.

**D7. `primary_position` is the most frequent event position in the match**, not the time weighted position. It is used for grouping only.

**D8. Provider possessions are split where control changes.** The provider's possession id can span a contested exchange: 91 shots (15 goals) were taken by a team other than the possession team, so labeling by provider id alone credited them to the wrong side. A sequence is a provider possession split at changes in the team performing ball actions (pass, carry, shot). Sequences with no ball action by their own team are dropped. After the split, sequence shots equal event shots in every team match and sequence goals equal final scores. 26 percent of provider possessions split into more than one sequence. Half of all sequences have 3 or fewer ball actions, so step 7 should consider a minimum length filter as a sensitivity check.

**D9. Two outcome schemes, run side by side as requested.** `strict`: turnover means a live turnover only; ball out of play and fouls are censored. `broad`: dead ball losses to the opponent also count as turnovers. Shot and goal labels are identical in both. Own restarts, live continuations, and period ends are censored in both. Usable shares: strict 68 percent, broad 81 percent.

**D10. Minutes normalization.** Volume style measures (strength, betweenness, PageRank, HITS) have a per 90 form, value times 90 over minutes. Season values use the ratio of sums, which equals the minutes weighted mean. Clustering and partner counts are not rescaled. This is a linear approximation, so step 8 will add a minimum minutes filter and test sensitivity to it.

**D11. Network measures use completed open play passes by default.** Edges are completed passes with a known recipient, excluding dead ball restarts. The all passes scope is stored for sensitivity; the player level PageRank per 90 rankings under the two scopes have Spearman correlation 0.98.

**D12. Web app brief (step 11), set by the author on 2026 10 06.** Minimalist, interactive, usability first, elegance and simplicity over flashiness. Serves precomputed season and player aggregates only; the author will confirm the StatsBomb terms before anything is published.

**D13. Markov chain construction (step 7).** The chain state is the zone where each ball action starts, on a 12 by 8 grid. A sequence is a path of those zones cut at its first shot, ending in goal or shot from the shot itself, or in goal or turnover from the sequence label when there is no shot. Sequences with a censored label keep their moves but add no terminal transition. Four configurations are run: strict or broad turnover, crossed with open play only (sequences not starting from a dead ball) or all sequences. Absorption probabilities solve (I minus Q) B equals R. Two value functions are kept: probability of a goal (`goal`) and of any shot or goal (`attempt`).

**D14. Action value.** Successful passes and carries earn V(end) minus V(start). A failed pass is charged minus V(start). Shots and dead ball restarts are not valued. The first player rankings are plausible (Cazorla, Ozil, Carrick, Hazard, Mahrez near the top). Split half reliability for players is moderate (0.65 to 0.84), so step 8 should treat player xT as noisy and prefer team level sums as features.

**D15. Synthetic sample is generated, not committed.** `make sample` writes a seeded synthetic league in StatsBomb JSON format (about 27 MB) and runs the unchanged pipeline on it. Generating instead of committing keeps the repository small and removes any doubt about licensing, and a test checks that two runs with the same seed are byte identical. The sample passes all 37 validation checks. A tiny end to end test (6 teams, 30 matches) runs in the normal test suite.

**D16. Step 8 design, fixed before looking at results.** Target: team match goal difference, with xG difference as the second target. Models: ridge regression with the penalty chosen by leave one out over matches inside each training fold, median imputation, standardization, and clipping at 5 standard deviations (a tiny network in a short window otherwise dominates a fit). Each match enters the fit once, from the home perspective, because the away row is its exact mirror, so both team rows are always in the same fold. Features are own minus opponent for the team match. Baselines: shots (Corsi style), shots plus shots on target, shots plus xG (goal difference target only), shots plus pass volume. Extensions: network features (12 structure measures plus pass volume), xT features (goal and attempt values), and both. Primary analysis: whole match features, 10 fold grouped CV repeated 10 times, open play networks, strict open play chain. Zone values for xT are refit inside every training fold from per match transition counts, so held out matches never touch them. A leaky run (values fit on all matches) is kept as a demonstration and changes results by less than 0.001. Uncertainty: paired bootstrap over matches of the change in R2. All seeds are in `config/project.yaml` under `modeling`.

**D17. Step 8 results and what they mean.** See `docs/results_step8.md`. Same match, network features add 0.17 R2 over shots for goal difference, xT adds 0.10, both 0.24, robust to CV scheme, season half, pass scope, dismissal filter, sequence length, and chain configuration. The network gain does not carry to the xG target, does not appear when features come from half 1 and the target from half 2, and is not significant while the score is level. We read it as a score effect on passing rather than evidence of predictive value, and say so in the report. xT is the more credible addition because it also explains xG difference. No chain configuration wins; strict open play stays the default. A forecasting test using previous matches is the recommended follow up.

**D18. Hockey data decision (made at the end of step 8, reversible).** Proceed with a limited hockey adapter in step 10 using the Stathletes Big Data Cup 2021 repository (`bigdatacup/Big-Data-Cup-2021`, women's Olympic 2022, NCAA and NWHL event files). Public pages show pass events with an intended recipient (`Player 2`), release and target coordinates from the eventing team perspective, and a legal note allowing research with public sharing of findings but no commercial use or resale. Gaps: no possession ids (we derive them as the data contract allows), number of games not verified, the author must accept the terms on GitHub before downloading, and the later editions carry competition use restrictions. It is not NHL data and likely too small for the incremental test, so hockey is a portability demonstration and descriptive network analysis, not a second answer to the research question. All Three Zones is not used: paid subscription, zone transition focus, no confirmed pass recipients, and it could not back a public app. Teammate Eric Winiecke's idea of extracting possession from game film is out of scope, since the pipeline only needs event data.

**D19. Local explorer app (author decision, 2026 10 07).** The author wants an interactive way to see the results and will run it only on their own machine, so the page uses match level data and is not published. It is a static page (`app/`) with no server and no network access: `scripts/11_build_app_data.py` writes `app/data.js` from the processed tables, which stays git ignored. Three views: Networks (any match, scrub by minute, live PageRank, betweenness, HITS and clustering from `app/metrics.js`, animated pass flow), Zones (zone values, a ball drop sampling the real season transition counts, a pass value calculator), and Evidence (the step 8 forest plot, a diagram of which minutes feed each window, and a binned relationship explorer). The relationship explorer uses xT zone values fit on the whole season, so it is exploratory and not a held out result. This replaces the earlier plan for a public app limited to season aggregates (D12); that plan returns if the app is ever published, because the StatsBomb terms still apply to anything shared.

**D20. Live play view (author feedback, 2026 10 07).** The first Networks view smoothed node positions toward window averages and drew aggregated links with random particles, so players seemed to drift and lines had no visible cause. It is replaced by two views on one clock. Live play animates the logged ball: each pass lasts until the recipient's logged reception (within 10 s, otherwise distance over 15 m/s), carries last until the player's next action, and times are spread by at least 0.1 s so every action can animate. Players are drawn at the place of their last logged action and glide to the place of their next one over 1.8 s; opposing players use the same rule rotated 180 degrees into the team's frame and are labelled estimates, since StatsBomb open data has no tracking and no 360 frames for this season. Network fixes nodes at match average positions and counts a pass when its pulse lands. Pass by pass time gives each action the same screen time and skips idle gaps. The payload now carries a per match feed `ev` (about 850,000 actions) and lineups `lu`, replacing the older per side pass arrays. Network node positions have three layouts (author request): Assigned uses a textbook spot per StatsBomb position name (`SLOTS` in `appdata/build.py`, each player's most frequent position in the match), Actual uses the average touch position, and Blend (default) is halfway. Assigned and blended spots are nudged apart to at least 7 m, which also separates a substitute from the player he replaced.

**D21. Evidence tab wording pass (author feedback, 2026 10 16).** The tab was hard to read for people who do not know the jargon, so this pass changes words and layout only. No statistic, model, `build.py` or `data.js` field changed, and every number on screen is still read from the step 8 comparison rows. Visible labels now use plain wording with the technical term as a tooltip or a small secondary label (share of goal difference explained, with R2 in the tooltip). Each term is a button: hover or keyboard focus shows a one line meaning and selecting it opens a glossary drawer with the longer entry. A one sentence takeaway sits at the top of the tab and of each card. The takeaways for the forest plot and the minutes card are built from the rows for the chosen target, baseline and window, and the top sentence is checked against the data before it is shown. Verdict rule: Clear gain when the whole 95% interval is above zero, Clear loss when it is wholly below zero, No clear gain otherwise. Only the interval decides, never the point estimate. An interval end within 0.0005 of zero is marked borderline, which currently happens for level score to full time with both feature sets (lower end +0.0002). The minutes card now tells the story in order: same match gain, the gain fading when features and goals come from different minutes, then the score effect as one possible reading, stated as consistent with the data and not proven. Step 3 only claims the pattern if the numbers show it. The relationship explorer says in plain words what left over after shots means, that the straight line is fitted on all matches, that its xT values are fitted on the whole season, and that it is exploratory and not a held out result. It also follows the minutes chosen in the card above, and now says so. The honest caveats stay on screen: one season, 380 matches, a same match association and not a forecast, no causal claim, low power for the half 1 to half 2 test, and 17 overlapping settings without a multiplicity correction. The wording module `app/evidence_text.js` has no DOM code so Node can test it (`node app/tests_js/run_evidence_text.js`). A browser check against a fixture runs with `python app/tests_js/make_evidence_fixture.py OUT.js` and `node app/tests_js/check_evidence_page.js OUT.js`. The fixture uses the real comparison rows and synthetic match arrays.
